## Markets & Macro

### The Budget lands on Wednesday

On Wednesday 28 October, Chancellor John Healey delivers his first Budget, and the first under Prime Minister Andy Burnham. It's the earliest Budget since 2021.

**What we know so far**

- **The message.** Healey has promised a Budget that moves "money and power out of Westminster" and is "built on fiscal discipline". The second phrase is aimed squarely at bond markets.
- **The constraint.** UK borrowing costs recently hit a 28-year high, so there is very little room to spend without spooking investors.
- **Tax.** Labour's manifesto rules out raising income tax, VAT or National Insurance rates. Expect frozen tax thresholds instead, which quietly raise revenue as wages rise.
- **Jobs.** Healey has talked a lot about apprenticeships and getting young people into work, so expect youth employment measures.
- **The forecast.** The Office for Budget Responsibility (OBR) publishes its new growth and borrowing forecasts the same day. Watch these as closely as the speech.

**Why it matters to you:** frozen thresholds mean more of your future graduate salary gets taxed, and youth employment schemes could shape the entry-level job market you're applying into.

> **Say it in the interview**
>
> "The Budget's challenge is credibility. With borrowing costs high, the Chancellor has to show markets he can fund his plans without raising headline tax rates."

## The Deal Desk

### Biossil becomes a unicorn

| Raised | Valuation | Lead investor | HQ |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $153M | $1B | OpenAI Startup Fund | Toronto |

**The deal:** Toronto biotech Biossil raised $153 million in September, in a round led by the OpenAI Startup Fund. The round values the company at $1 billion, making it Canada's newest unicorn.

**What they do:** many drugs fail in clinical trials and get shelved by the pharma companies that made them. Biossil uses AI language models to scan these discarded candidates, find the promising ones, then buy or license them and take them to market itself.

**The business model:** instead of discovering drugs from scratch, which takes many years and costs a fortune, Biossil starts with molecules that have already been part-tested. That gives it a head start. Revenue comes later, from selling or licensing drugs that succeed.

**Why investors care:** it's an AI story with a real-world payoff. Founders Fund, Duke University's endowment and the Abu Dhabi Investment Council joined OpenAI in the round. The money goes into clinical development and buying more drug candidates.

**The red flag:** these drugs failed once already. Clinical trials are slow, expensive and uncertain, and AI doesn't change what regulators require. Biossil could be years away from meaningful revenue.

> **Learning objective: what makes a unicorn?**
>
> A unicorn is a private startup valued at $1 billion or more. That value isn't based on profit. It's set by the price investors pay for a slice of the company. If investors pay $153 million for roughly 15% of Biossil, the whole company is implied to be worth about $1 billion. That's why valuations can rise fast on hype and fall just as fast.

## Tech Pulse

### Is AI eating the entry-level job?

Companies are hiring fewer graduates into the routine roles AI now handles, and that's the job market you're applying into.

**The evidence**

- An Anthropic report from March 2026 found employment for 22–25 year olds fell 3–16% in the roles most exposed to AI. The cause was fewer new hires, not layoffs.
- In a survey of US hiring managers, 45% said one senior worker using AI now does the work of several entry-level staff. Nearly half said they'd rather invest in AI than train a graduate.

**Not everyone agrees.** Accenture says it's hiring *more* entry-level people in 2026 than in 2025, betting that graduates bring AI fluency. PwC has gone the other way and cut US entry-level hiring.

**The business logic:** cutting graduate intake saves money now. But if firms stop training juniors, who becomes their senior talent in five years?

**What to do about it:** show you can *direct and check* AI output, not just use it. A small project, such as automating part of a task and explaining what the AI got wrong, makes a strong application example.

*Many of these figures come from US surveys run by recruitment and CV websites, so treat them as signals rather than hard data.*

## Career Corner

### "Tell me about a business story you've followed"

This comes up in almost every placement interview, and most people answer it badly by just summarising the news. Interviewers want to hear you think. Use this four-step structure:

1. **What happened:** one or two sentences, with a number if you have one.
2. **Why it happened:** the cause or the incentive behind it.
3. **Who wins and who loses:** companies, sectors, consumers.
4. **Your view:** what you think happens next, and why.

> **Example answer, using this week's Deal Desk**
>
> "Biossil, a Toronto biotech, raised $153 million at a $1 billion valuation, led by OpenAI's startup fund. It uses AI to find failed drug candidates worth reviving, which cuts the cost and time of starting from scratch. Pharma companies could benefit by selling off shelved assets, but clinical trials are still slow and uncertain. I think the valuation is a bet on AI hype as much as results, so the next trial outcomes will decide whether it holds."

**Your task this week:** pick one story from this issue and practise your answer out loud. Aim for 60–90 seconds.

## Lesson of the week

### Fiscal drag

**Fiscal drag** is when the government raises more tax without changing tax rates, by freezing the income thresholds where each rate starts. As wages rise with inflation, more people get pulled into higher tax bands. It's a quiet tax rise, and it's likely to come up on Wednesday.

> **Use it in a sentence**
>
> "Frozen thresholds create fiscal drag, which lets the Chancellor raise revenue without breaking the manifesto pledge on tax rates."
